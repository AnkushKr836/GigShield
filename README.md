## Scope

### What GigShield is

A **B2B2C parametric insurance model**: delivery companies (Zomato, Swiggy, Zepto, and similar platforms) purchase a coverage plan on behalf of their riders. Riders themselves pay nothing — they're automatically covered the moment they register under a company with an active plan.

When a rider's delivery is disrupted by something outside their control — heavy rain, flooding, a curfew, a road closure — they can raise a claim against that specific delivery. The system checks the claim against objective, pre-recorded disruption data for that zone and time window, rather than relying purely on the rider's word. If a match is found, the claim is approved automatically and a payout amount is calculated from the company's coverage tier. If no match is found, the claim is queued for manual review instead of being rejected outright.

### What GigShield deliberately excludes

- **Health, accident, life, or vehicle-repair claims** — coverage is strictly limited to income loss caused by external disruptions.
- **Real payment processing** — approved claims show a calculated payout amount, but no money actually moves.
- **Real weather or traffic data** — all disruption events are fabricated for demonstration purposes (see [How it works](#how-it-works)).
- **Fraud detection or ML-based scoring** — out of scope for this phase; the claim-decision logic is a straightforward rule check (does a matching disruption record exist for this ride's time and zone), not a trained model.

### Why fabricated data

This is a class prototype meant to demonstrate the *mechanics* of parametric insurance — verification against objective data, automated decisioning, tiered payouts — without depending on external API keys, real company partnerships, or a live payment gateway. Every rider's ride history and every disruption event in the system is generated by the application itself, on demand, so the full flow can be explored end-to-end with zero external setup.

=======
1. **Registration** — a rider signs up under a specific company and zone. No payment is collected.
2. **Ride history** — the rider generates a batch of fabricated completed deliveries (realistic locations, timestamps, and fares) via a single button. Alongside these rides, the system also seeds one fabricated disruption event guaranteed to overlap at least one of them.
3. **Raising a claim** — the rider opens any completed ride and submits a claim: what kind of disruption occurred (weather or civic), a description, and the amount they're claiming.
4. **Automated assessment** — the backend searches for a disruption event in that ride's zone, of the matching type, whose time window overlaps the ride. 
   - **Match found** → claim is **approved**, and the payout is set to the rider's company's coverage-plan rate.
   - **No match found** → claim is marked **manual review**, left open rather than presumed false.
5. **Dashboard** — the rider sees total earnings protected across all approved claims, alongside their ride and claim counts.

## Design system

GigShield uses a custom visual language rather than default component-library styling:

- **Color palette** — an "overcast sky" background with a "storm slate" primary and a single "beacon amber" accent color, chosen to evoke weather rather than a generic SaaS dashboard.
- **Typography** — Archivo for headings, Inter for body text, IBM Plex Mono for numeric readouts (money amounts, gauge values).
- **The Coverage Barometer** — a custom SVG arc gauge styled after a physical barometer, used on the dashboard to display total earnings protected. Chosen deliberately over a generic progress bar, since the product's entire premise is weather-triggered protection.

## Current limitations

This section is intentionally explicit, since this is a prototype rather than a finished product:

- No admin authentication on data-seeding endpoints (`/companies`, `/zones`, `/coverage-plans`) — anyone can create these right now.
- No real payout execution step — an approved claim shows a calculated amount, but no transaction record or money transfer occurs.
- No fraud detection or credibility scoring — the underlying data models exist but are not yet wired into the claim-decision flow.
- No real external data sources — all rides and disruption events are generated by the application itself.
- The navigation bar does not reflect whether a rider is currently logged in.

## Roadmap

GigShield is being built in three phases. **Phase 1 is complete** — a working end-to-end prototype covering registration, simulated rides, claim submission, and automated approval/review decisioning, wrapped in a coherent visual design.

### Phase 2 (planned)
- A visible payout step — approved claims generate an actual payout record with its own status, rather than just a calculated number.
- Basic admin views for managing companies, zones, and coverage plans through the UI instead of direct API calls.
- Session-aware navigation — the nav bar reflects whether a rider is logged in.
- A simple rule-based credibility score per rider (using existing but currently unused data models), factored into claim review priority.

### Phase 3 (planned)
- An analytics view surfacing claim trends and payout totals across companies and zones.
- Expanded automated test coverage across the API layer.
- A basic fraud-pattern check (e.g. flagging unusually frequent claims from a single rider), built on top of the ride and claim history already being tracked.
- General UI polish and consolidation of any remaining rough edges identified during Phase 1 and 2 demos.

Each phase continues to prioritize a working, demonstrable prototype over production-readiness — real external integrations (payment gateways, weather APIs) remain out of scope throughout.
